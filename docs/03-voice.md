# Voice

How Meritalien sounds. Grounded in the copy that is actually on the site — every example
below is real.

---

## The register

**Short, plain, confident. A person who knows the answer, telling you the answer.**

The homepage headline is seven words:

> Exceptional talent shouldn't be limited by **borders**.

That is the whole thesis and the whole tone at once. It states a position. It does not
qualify it, soften it, or explain why we're qualified to hold it.

### The two-beat line

The signature move. A statement, then a turn:

| Real line | The turn |
|---|---|
| "Talent is everywhere. Opportunity isn't." | `everywhere` → `isn't` |
| "Four layers. One journey." | `four` → `one` |
| "Two ends of a journey, and what you do." | the ends, then the reader |
| "People without borders." | the whole brand, in three words |
| "What you build is what unlocks the move." | mechanism, plainly |

Two sentences, or one sentence with a hard stop. The period is doing structural work —
it is not a comma with attitude. If you find yourself joining the two halves with "and"
or "because", you have turned a statement into an explanation.

### Say the number

Not "significant growth" — the number, its date, and its method. Not "a range of
routes" — the route names, from the official portal. If a fact cannot be sourced, it
does not go on the page.

## Rules

### 1. Plain nouns, human scale

Use: *talent, borders, journey, move, route, case, proof, work.*
Avoid: *solutions, leverage, ecosystem, holistic, seamless, cutting-edge, world-class.*

The site's word for what it does is **mobility**. The site's word for what you have is a
**route**. Neither is jargon.

### 2. Second person for anything the reader does

> "Start with the case you actually have."
> "Know where you can go."
> "Find your route."

*You* for the reader, *we* only when we are actually doing something. Never "the user",
never "one".

### 3. Zero hedging outside `/legal/`

This is a hard site-wide rule and it was repeated emphatically during the build.

- **No** "we are not a law firm" on a content page.
- **No** "in preparation", "coming soon", "may vary", "subject to change".
- **No** "this is not legal advice" except on the disclosures page.

All qualification lives in `/legal/` — disclosures, privacy, terms — and nowhere else.
A content page that hedges reads as a business that does not believe its own product.

If something genuinely cannot be stated, the move is not to soften it. The move is to
**say what you would measure instead**, or to omit it.

### 4. Customer language beats clever language. Always.

The reader is a founder, a researcher, a physician — someone with a real case and a real
deadline. They are not impressed by wordplay. If a line could appear on any company's
site, delete it. If a line requires a second read to decode, it is clever and it is
failing.

### 5. Earn the Serif

Newsreader is for statements. Geist is for structure, data and interface. Departure Mono
is for machine-facts — eyebrows, labels, city pairs.

Do not set a headline in serif to make it feel important. Do not set body copy in
serif because it looks editorial. The serif register is reserved, and reserving it is
what makes it mean something.

## Do / Don't

| ✗ Don't | ✓ Do |
|---|---|
| "We are a leading provider of global talent mobility solutions." | "Talent is everywhere. Opportunity isn't." |
| "Our comprehensive suite of services helps exceptional individuals navigate complex immigration pathways." | "Find your route." |
| "Significant improvements in processing times may be possible." | "O-1A: 2–4 months. EB-1A: 8–14 months." *(with a source)* |
| "Please note this does not constitute legal advice." | *(delete it — it belongs on `/legal/` only)* |
| "Unlock your potential with our innovative platform." | "Start with the case you actually have." |
| "A range of options are available depending on your circumstances." | "Four layers. One journey." |

## Register by surface

| Surface | Register | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | One position, stated | "Exceptional talent shouldn't be limited by borders." |
| Section heads | Two-beat, or a plain instruction | "Four layers. One journey." |
| Eyebrows | Machine-label, uppercase mono | "Global talent mobility" |
| Body | Direct address, short sentences | "Start with the case you actually have." |
| Buttons | Verb + object, two words | "Find your route" |
| Legal | Precise, hedged, complete | *(the only place hedging is correct)* |
| Stories | The subject's own facts | Named person, real origin, real route |

## The eyebrow vocabulary

Eyebrows are a fixed set of machine-labels, in mono, uppercase. They are not sentences
and they do not vary for variety's sake:

```
Global talent mobility     The system         The navigator
Discover                   Build              Connect              Move
For companies              Legal              Sources
```

An eyebrow that reads as a headline is a headline in the wrong place.

---

## The accent rule, in copy

The same discipline as the colour system: **one word per surface carries the emphasis.**
On the homepage it is "borders". It is set in serif italic, in the accent green.

If two things in a line are emphasised, neither is emphasised. Pick the one word the
reader must not miss.
